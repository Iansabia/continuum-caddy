Why it matters

A launch monitor costs thousands and lives at the range. A caddy who knows your game walks beside a lucky few. Your phone can be both.

The numbers that tell you how you really hit the ball, and the on-course judgment of a caddy who has watched you play, have always been expensive and scarce. Continuum reads both from the device every golfer already carries, so a smarter round does not require a simulator bay or a tour bag.

How it works

From a mounted phone to a caddy on every hole.

The pipeline, step by step: capture the swing, collect the ball frame by frame, estimate the shot, then your club and a landing zone. Illustrative.

1

Mount and hit

Clip your phone to the cart or a stand, on the range or the course, and take your normal swing. Nothing on the club, nothing to calibrate.

2

Vision reads the shot

Computer vision tracks your body and the ball frame by frame, catching the strike and the ball leaving the face.

3

Estimate the outcome

From the ball in those frames we estimate carry, ball speed, and launch, the way a launch monitor reports it.

4

Caddy on the course

Your read becomes advice: the club for the shot, the smart miss, and a ring on the map to help you find your ball.

Measured, not claimed

We say estimate until the data earns measure.

Phone-only shot reading is hard, and plenty of apps quote numbers they cannot back up. We do the opposite: every estimate is validated against a real launch monitor by capturing the same shots both ways, so the accuracy we show is earned, not asserted. Where the physics has a ceiling, we say so, spin, for instance, is read as tendency and axis, never invented as an exact rpm. Our caddy advice works the same way: it shows its reasoning, not just a number.

Be the caddy golfers actually trust, and do not claim we are there until the numbers back it up.
